# Affiliation, Morality, Karma, and Exposure

This document separates concepts that must not be collapsed into one reputation meter.

## Moral Alignment

Moral alignment is a continuous **Malicious ↔ Benevolent** axis.

Implementation scale:

```text
-100  = maximally malicious
   0  = morally neutral
+100  = maximally benevolent
```

Affiliation does not determine morality.

Examples that must remain possible:

- a malicious member of a Righteous faction,
- a benevolent Demonic cultivator,
- a ruthless Buddhist,
- a comparatively honorable Unorthodox cultivator,
- a corrupt Imperial official.

This distinction is essential because internal punishment and faction sanctions depend on what a member has actually become, not only their banner.

## Affiliations

Initial top-level affiliations:

- Unaffiliated
- Righteous
- Unorthodox
- Demonic
- Buddhist
- Imperial

These are political/cultural/cultivation affiliations, not a direct good/evil scale.

Specific sects, clans, empires, temples, cults, and organizations will later exist underneath these categories.

## Explicit Natural Relations

The first relation model records the currently defined world rules:

- **Righteous ↔ Unorthodox:** mortal enemies
- **Buddhist ↔ Demonic:** mortal enemies
- **Righteous ↔ Buddhist:** compatible
- **Righteous ↔ Imperial:** compatible
- **Unorthodox ↔ Demonic:** compatible
- **Buddhist ↔ Unorthodox:** hostile
- **Righteous ↔ Demonic:** hostile
- **Imperial ↔ Unorthodox:** hostile
- **Imperial ↔ Demonic:** hostile

Unspecified combinations remain neutral until worldbuilding defines them more precisely.

Specific factions can override these broad relations later.

## Internal Discipline

Affiliation penalties should be morality-aware.

### Righteous / Buddhist / Imperial

Punishing or killing a member who has become **malicious (alignment < 0)** can be legitimate and should not automatically trigger normal same-faction murder sanctions.

Honor, virtue, law, karma, witnesses, authority, and quest context can still matter.

### Demonic

A Demonic cultivator who becomes strongly benevolent can be regarded as having gone soft, betrayed the path, or become unworthy.

Killing such a member can therefore be tolerated more readily than killing a properly Demonic member.

Demonic organizations are still not consequence-free social spaces; useful members are valuable investments.

### Unorthodox

Internal violence is much more normalized. Same-affiliation killing carries fewer inherent moral/social consequences than in orderly factions.

Specific gangs, clans, brotherhoods, and leaders can still enforce their own rules.

## Energy Exposure

Affiliation is not only a menu label.

Cultivation methods carry signatures. NPCs can detect them through:

- visible aura,
- active techniques,
- Burst,
- weapon/armor infusion,
- spiritual perception,
- deliberate cultivation,
- residual Qi,
- injuries/contamination,
- specialized detection techniques.

Using incompatible techniques in hostile territory can expose the player's real cultivation path.

Examples:

- Demonic Qi exposed in a Righteous settlement can provoke lethal action.
- Buddhist cultivation exposed in Demonic territory can provoke lethal action.
- Calm seated cultivation in view of hostile or suspicious NPCs can reveal the cultivator and trigger an attack.

Concealment techniques, treasures, formations, suppressed circulation, false identities, and low output can later interact with this detection system.

## Demonic Cultivation

Demonic cultivation is deliberately high-risk/high-reward.

Its harshness is structural:

- brutal training,
- lethal failure states,
- dangerous power shortcuts,
- life-force expenditure,
- Qi theft/drain,
- self-damage,
- instability,
- possible minor-stage regression in severe cultivation disasters.

Demonic organizations invest in members who prove useful, but failure can be fatal.

### Control and Berserk Risk

Demonic techniques require control.

Rapid advancement without corresponding control is dangerous. Talented cultivators can therefore become dangerous to themselves because they gain Demonic power faster than they learn to govern it.

Berserk events are **not random ambient punishment**. They arise from a combination of relevant conditions such as:

- Demonic technique instability,
- poor control/mastery,
- excessive Burst,
- injuries,
- Qi deviation,
- contaminated circulation,
- life-force expenditure,
- forced breakthroughs.

A sufficiently bad failure can:

- injure the cultivator,
- consume life force,
- trigger uncontrollable aggression,
- temporarily boost combat power,
- damage cultivation,
- in severe cases regress a minor stage.

Demonic cultivators can reduce this risk by:

- training weaker Demonic techniques first,
- repeatedly practicing control,
- advancing professions,
- using stabilizing resources,
- improving vessel quality,
- avoiding reckless Burst/forced breakthroughs.

## Buddhist Karma

Buddhist cultivation uses Karma as an additional progression/judgment system.

Karma is not identical to the universal moral-alignment value.

Actions that should harm Buddhist Karma include:

- killing ordinary villagers,
- killing protected/peaceful animals such as foxes, bears, and pandas,
- aiding Demonic cultivators in ways contrary to Buddhist principles,
- harming virtuous members without justification.

Buddhist paths should lean toward support, restraint, purification, defense, healing, sealing, and other virtue-compatible methods, without forcing every Buddhist build into a healer role.

Defeating clearly malicious targets can be Karma-positive or otherwise spiritually beneficial depending on circumstance.

## Cross-Affiliation Cooperation

Players may create groups across affiliations, but cooperation has consequences.

Examples:

- Buddhist aid to a Demonic cultivator can incur severe Karma costs.
- Demonic aid to Buddhists can impose Demonic-path penalties/debuffs.
- Righteous and Unorthodox players can cooperate, but quests/actions against their own side can generate dissatisfaction, reputation loss, or sanctions.
- Same-affiliation punishment can be acceptable when the target's moral alignment has crossed the faction's tolerated boundary.

The party/group system therefore must not erase affiliation politics.
